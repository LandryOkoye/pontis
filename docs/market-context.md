# Market context

- **Status:** research snapshot. Every third-party fact carries a source tag `[S#]`; the sources list at the end gives the URL, and every source was accessed on **2026-11-11**.
- **Scope of the claims:** Pontis is a testnet project on synthetic assets ([README](../README.md), [ADR 0014](decisions/0014-scope-expansion-beyond-mvp.md)). Nothing here says Pontis has users, liquidity or real backing. It does not.
- **Convention:** a claim that could not be checked against a primary page is marked `UNVERIFIED`. Market figures move daily and the two market-size sources below use different counting rules, so the numbers are a range, not a fact.

The overview tab we read was not labelled "Distributed" in the extracted text, so the ranking basis is `UNVERIFIED`.

### Fragmentation across issuers

- **Many issuers, one underlying.** On the rwa.xyz stocks page, the largest issuers by value are bStocks (104 tokens, $1.5B), Ondo (433, $906.8M), xStocks (1,498, $699.5M), Securitize (27, $236.8M), Reality (3,237, $171.3M), Robinhood (198, $149.8M), Coinbase (307, $44.0M) and Superstate Opening Bell (3, $40.5M) [S3]. Bitquery counted 12,062 stock tokens from six issuers on seven chains [S4].
- **The same company appears as separate, non-swappable tokens.** Circle appears as `CRCLB` (bStocks, BNB Chain, $148.5M) and `CRCLon` (Ondo, $80.0M); Strategy appears as `MSTRB` (bStocks) and `STRCx` (xStocks) [S1] [S3]. Bitquery names Circle as the most-tokenized company across all six issuers [S4]. A holder of one cannot exchange it for another at a published parity; they sell and buy back, as the Unison hackathon team also states [S20].
- **The tokens are not legally the same claim.** Ondo tokens are total-return trackers: one token does not necessarily represent one share and the token price diverges from the stock over time [S5]. Robinhood stock tokens are "tokenised debt securities" issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited that give economic exposure and no rights against the underlying issuer [S16]. xStocks are described as backed 1:1 by the underlying held in regulated custody [S9]. Superstate describes legal shares of public companies, tokenized [S11]. A parity ratio between any two of these needs a reviewer to accept the basis risk.
- **Unit conventions differ and break easily.** Robinhood and Securitize use an on-chain display multiplier (ERC-8056 `uiMultiplier`) for dividends and splits [S16] [S12]; Ondo uses a display multiplier on Solana and BNB Chain and shows a per-token price change on Ethereum [S5]. Bitquery found that, for nine Robinhood EU tokens, the on-chain change after a split was the square of the split ratio [S4].
- **Wrappers add a second claim.** Centrifuge's deRWA format wraps a permissioned fund share (JAAA) into a freely transferable note (deJAAA). A paid research memo says the note's issuer is a different entity, Open Market Factory Limited, whose terms are not public, so the holder's claim is on the note issuer and not the fund share [S28] (secondary source, one reviewer's judgement).
- **Treasury exposure is fragmented but not interchangeable.** The largest tokenized Treasury products are different funds (USDY $2.4B, USYC $2.1B, iBENJI $1.7B, BUIDL $1.7B, BENJI $1.2B) [S1]. These are `ECONOMICALLY_SIMILAR` at best under Pontis's vocabulary, not `DIRECT_EQUIVALENT`. The strict same-claim case is much narrower than the headline number.

### Liquidity fragmentation

- Ondo's own documentation says tokenized-stock liquidity is "recreated onchain, separate from the deep pools" of the underlying markets, which leads to thin books, slippage and price dislocations unless minting and redemption are cheap enough to arbitrage [S6].
- Liquidity also sits inside venues. 88% of Binance's bStocks, which are 41% of all counted tokenized stock value, sit in five Binance wallets held for its users [S4].
- Ondo sets no mint fee and a 0.05% redemption fee, with instant atomic mint and burn for KYC-approved users [S5]. That is the price a parity conversion has to beat for a user who can already sell and buy back through the issuer.

### Issuer-controlled transfer restrictions

- Superstate: a wallet must be added to an allowlist to hold tokenized shares in a third-party wallet [S11].
- Securitize DS Protocol: a registry of investors and a compliance service decide who can hold; the issuer role can mint, burn, seize and lock tokens and the master role can pause trading [S12].
- Ondo: direct mint and redemption need KYC; US persons, Canada and several sanctioned jurisdictions are prohibited and other jurisdictions need professional or accredited status [S5] [S7].
- xStocks: not available to residents of the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia or sanctioned jurisdictions [S9]. Robinhood: only authorised participants can subscribe, and offers to US persons are not allowed [S16].
- The restrictions are common enough that Uniswap Labs shipped a hook standard for allowlisted pools, with Superstate and Securitize as launch partners [S13] [S14].

### Cross-chain gaps

- Ondo tokens are on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana, and "select tokens" are bridgeable to HyperEVM and Robinhood Chain [S5]. xStocks list eleven chains [S9]. Robinhood runs one token set for EU users on Arbitrum and another on its own Robinhood Chain [S4].
- Bridged tokens stay in each chain's count and leave the issuers' creation and redemption figures, which makes cross-chain supply hard to audit from outside [S4].
- Moving between chains and between issuers are two different problems. Most infrastructure below solves the first for one token; the second (issuer A's token to issuer B's token) has few published solutions. That is the gap Pontis targets, with the caveat in the table notes.

## (c) What would have to be true for Pontis to matter

1. **A defensible class of equivalent pairs exists.** Today the major tokens are different legal claims (total-return tracker, debt security, 1:1 backed claim, legal share) [S5] [S16] [S9] [S11]. Pontis matters only if a reviewer can honestly approve some pairs as `DIRECT_EQUIVALENT` at a stated ratio, and the cases where that is true (same underlying and the same backing model, or a permissioned token and its wrapper from one issuer) are common enough to matter.
2. **Issuers cooperate or at least tolerate.** Superstate and Securitize tokens need allowlisted holders [S11] [S12]. A Pontis vault or pool account has to be allowlisted by each issuer, or Pontis stays a synthetic demo.
3. **Users switch issuers often enough to pay for it.** With free mint and a 5 bps redemption fee at one major issuer [S5], a conversion has to cost less than sell-and-rebuy plus spread to be worth using. For scale, Unison charges 2 bps base [S20]. Whether the real pain is price, tax events (Unison's claim, `UNVERIFIED` [S20]) or custody lock-in is not measured.
4. **Fragmentation persists.** It may not. One issuer already holds 41% of counted value five months after launch [S4]; a standard multiplier (ERC-8056 appears in Robinhood and Securitize code [S16] [S12]) and aggregators such as NEAR Intents and 1inch can make switching cheap without a parity protocol [S5] [S17].
5. **Someone supplies two-sided inventory and bears depeg risk.** The MVP uses a controlled vault funded by the operator ([ADR 0008](decisions/0008-vault-inventory-and-funding.md)). A real market needs LPs who accept basis risk between wrappers with different claims. Unison's answer is a peg guard at 50 bps [S20]; Pontis's equivalent (workstream B) is not built.
6. **Reserve and attestation data is trustworthy.** Supply is not outstanding value [S4]. A converter that trusts an issuer's reported supply or reserves can hold a token that is not backed. Daily third-party attestation as Ondo describes [S8] is the bar; Pontis's attester roles (workstream C and D) are not built.
7. **The regulatory position works.** Whether operating a conversion between security-like tokens is a regulated activity in any jurisdiction has not been assessed. `UNVERIFIED`; the project is testnet and synthetic assets only for this reason.

## (d) Where we are weak

- **Everything is synthetic.** Two test tokens we control, one made-up claim (`SYN-GOLD-OZ`), no real issuer, backing or users ([README](../README.md)). The MVP shows a mechanism, not a market.
- **One chain with little RWA liquidity.** The value sits on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana; NEAR is not in the rwa.xyz top ten [S1]. Reach depends on the bridge and Intents work, which is unbuilt; the Chain Signatures and Intents research files are still lists of questions ([research notes](research/near-intents.md)).
- **Trust is centralised.** Quotes are signed by one backend key; a stolen key lets an attacker sign quotes the contract would honour at its enforced ratio and fee ([architecture overview](architecture/overview.md)). Equivalence is a human reviewer's opinion; ADR 0014 section 6 lists every trust assumption this adds.
- **Equivalence review does not scale.** The MVP has one approved pair. rwa.xyz lists 6,776 stock tokens [S3]. Per-pair human review across thousands of tokens is not a plan; a rules-based pre-screen would be needed and does not exist.
- **Someone else is ahead on the idea.** Unison implements parity conversion with LPs and a dark pool on public testnets [S20]. Issuers can also build their own conversion or bridge paths (Ondo already bridges select tokens [S5]).
- **No demand evidence.** No user interviews, no pilot, no measurement of how often holders want to switch issuers. The thresholds in [success-metrics.md](success-metrics.md) are proposals, not benchmarks.
- **Dependence on issuer cooperation and on third-party integrations that can change terms,** for example Ondo access through NEAR Intents remains subject to issuer eligibility rules [S19].
- **Not audited, not legally reviewed, not on mainnet.**
